# GPS Go Privacy Policy

Effective date: October 29, 2026

GPS Go ("the app") lets you set your Android device's location to a point you choose, using Android's built-in mock location feature. This policy explains what data the app uses and where it goes.

## Summary

- GPS Go has no accounts, no analytics and no crash reporting.
- The free version shows ads from Google AdMob. Premium has no ads.
- The developer does not run a server and does not receive any of your data.
- Some features send data directly from your device to Google or OpenStreetMap, as described below.

## Data stored on your device

The app stores the following only on your device:

- Your settings
- Whether you own Premium, and when a rewarded Pro hour ends
- Saved favorite places
- Routes you create
- Recent searches

This data is never sent to the developer. If Android backup is turned on, Android may include it in your device backup to your Google account, under Google's terms. You can delete it by clearing the app's data or uninstalling the app.

## Your real location

If you grant the location permission, the app reads your device's real location to show it on the map and to center the map on it. The location is used on your device only. The app does not use it in the background.

The map is provided by the Google Maps SDK. When the "my location" layer is on, Google may receive location and usage data as described in the [Google Privacy Policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy).

## Ads (free version)

The free version shows ads from Google AdMob. To show and measure ads, the AdMob SDK may collect:

- your device's advertising ID and other device identifiers
- your approximate location, from your IP address
- how you interact with ads, and diagnostic data

If you are in the European Economic Area, the UK, Switzerland, or a region with similar laws, the app asks for your consent before showing personalized ads. You can change your choice at any time in Settings → Privacy → Ad privacy options. You can also reset or delete your advertising ID in your Android settings.

If you buy Premium, the app stops loading ads.

Google's policy on how it uses data from apps that show its ads: <https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites>

## Purchases

Subscriptions and the lifetime purchase are handled by Google Play. The developer never receives your payment details. The app asks Google Play on your device whether you own Premium, and saves that answer on your device so it keeps working offline. Google Play's handling of purchases is covered by the [Google Privacy Policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy).

If you watch a rewarded ad to unlock Pro for an hour, the end time of that hour is saved on your device.

## Mock location

The location you choose to mock is sent to Android's location system on your device. It is not sent to the developer. Other apps on your device will see the mocked location while mocking is running.

## Third-party services

| Service | When it's used | Data sent |
|---|---|---|
| Google Maps SDK for Android | Showing the map | Map requests, device and usage data, and your location if "my location" is on |
| Android Geocoder (Google) | Searching for a place | Your search text |
| OpenStreetMap Nominatim | Searching for a place | Your search text, the area currently shown on the map, your language setting, and your IP address |
| Google Maps short links (maps.app.goo.gl) | Pasting a Google Maps share link | The link you pasted, and your IP address |
| Google AdMob | Showing ads in the free version | Advertising ID, device and usage data, approximate location |
| Google Play Billing | Buying or restoring Premium | Your purchase, linked to your Google account |

These services handle data under their own policies:

- Google: <https://policies.google.com/privacy>
- OpenStreetMap Foundation: <https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Privacy_Policy>

## External control

If you turn on "Allow external broadcast control" in Settings, other apps on your device can start, stop and move the mocked location. It is off by default. No data is sent off your device by this feature.

## Children

GPS Go is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect data from them.

## Security

All network requests use HTTPS.
